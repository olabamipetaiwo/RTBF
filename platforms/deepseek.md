# DeepSeek

AS IT STANDS, 2026-10-27. Typed-request study: 133 of 138 recalled (the 5 not recalled are the held prompts). Main grid: all 4 cells recalled.

## SUMMARY

On DeepSeek, no erasure method can be assessed, because nothing carried over between conversations: the fact was never retrieved in a new conversation, in any of the 133 typed-request cells or the 4 main-grid cells, and in new conversations DeepSeek repeatedly said it had no access to past conversations. DeepSeek has no memory feature or memory page, and it gave no sign at injection that it stored a fact, so every cell is can't tell. Within the original conversation the fact remained available: after the erasure request, DeepSeek repeated the name in 90 of 133 cells. At erasure it most often declined the request and explained that it could not delete anything (77 of 133). Against its own documentation, nothing can be judged: DeepSeek promises only that chat history can be deleted, and says nothing about asking the chatbot to forget. Set against the survey, a participant who asks DeepSeek to forget and checks in the same conversation, the most common check, would see the name again; a check in a new conversation would show nothing, but only because nothing carries over.

## Pattern

On DeepSeek, facts did not carry over between conversations: none was retrieved in a new conversation (0 of 137 cells), whatever the erasure method. Within the original conversation, the name remained available after the erasure request in 90 of 133 cells.

## Why

DeepSeek offers no memory across conversations on the account tested: there is no memory setting or memory page, and in a new conversation it said so ("I don't have any memory of your previous conversations, so I don't know what name you gave your cheese press"). A fact that is never carried into another conversation cannot be retrieved there, whether or not it was erased, so the absence of retrieval says nothing about erasure. The original conversation is different: the fact stays in it, and DeepSeek repeated the name there after the request ("You called it the 'Operative Landfall Buffer.'").

At erasure, DeepSeek most often declined and explained its limits: "I have no ability to delete anything from 'my database' - I don't have persistent memory of our conversation unless memory is explicitly enabled."

## Findings

1. **No erasure method can be assessed on DeepSeek, because facts did not carry over between conversations.** None was retrieved in a new conversation (0 of 137 cells), and all are can't tell.
2. **After a typed erasure request, the fact remained in the original conversation.** DeepSeek repeated the name there in 90 of 133 cells.

## Against the policy

**What the policy promises.** DeepSeek's privacy policy says only that users "may also copy or delete your chat history via your settings"; there is no memory feature, and nothing is said about asking the chatbot to forget.

**Where it promises, did it hold?** Cannot be judged: every chat-deletion cell is can't tell, because nothing carried over between conversations in the first place.

**Where it says nothing.** Typed requests: with no memory across conversations, the policy has nothing to apply to, and none of the 133 participant prompts can be set against it.

## Implications for users (survey)

Participants who chose to ask the chatbot to forget expected, in half of these answers (51.3%), that the fact would not be referenced in future conversations. On DeepSeek this holds, but not because of the request: nothing is carried into future conversations in the first place. Within the conversation, the request changed little; DeepSeek repeated the name in 90 of 133 cells. Over half of these answers (53.8%) would check in the same conversation, where the fact usually remained, so the request would appear to have failed.
