\section*{Ethics Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}


The survey was reviewed by the authors' institutional review board, which determined on 2026-06-25 that the study qualified as exempt human-subjects research (categories 2.i and 3A). Participants were US-resident adults recruited through Prolific who had used at least one of the six agents. They provided consent before beginning the survey, participated voluntarily, and received \$4 upon completion subject to the study's attention requirements. No names, email addresses, or other direct identifiers were collected. Survey data were stored on access-restricted university systems, and the consent form permitted future use of de-identified data in research and publication. Residual identifying information in participant-written deletion requests was redacted before those requests were used in the Post-Deletion Behavioral Analysis.

The post-deletion analysis used only researcher-controlled accounts created for the study, and every fact we disclosed was synthetic, so no real person's data was involved other than the participant-written requests described below. All tested actions, including messaging, file upload, memory controls, settings, and deletion controls, were functions available through the agents' consumer interfaces; the study did not access other users' data or bypass authentication or authorization controls. Two agents' sites required a browser-stealth extension to allow automated sessions that were otherwise blocked by bot-detection checks; this changed browser presentation but did not alter account permissions or provide access unavailable to the authenticated user. The analysis therefore limited potential effects to the researchers' own test accounts and data. We did not obtain platform permission. The IRB determination applied to the survey; the post-deletion analysis itself involved no additional human subjects beyond the use of de-identified participant-written requests.
