# Schaub, Balebako, Durity, Cranor. A Design Space for Effective Privacy Notices

SOUPS 2015 (Symposium on Usable Privacy and Security), Ottawa, July 22-24, 2015, USENIX. Page range as in the manifest (not rechecked here). File: `4_users/schaub-soups15-privacy-notices-design-space.pdf`. Read the abstract, introduction, the statement of contributions and the sections that define the notice dimensions in outline (timing, channel, modality, control). I did not read every example notice or the per-domain discussion.

## Question
What are the design choices for a privacy notice, and how can they be organised so that designers can reason about notice and control for a given system (Abstract)?

## Method
- Systematization of knowledge: a survey of the literature on privacy notices to identify challenges, requirements and best practices, then a design space of privacy notices with dimensions (timing, channel, modality and the control options a notice offers), illustrated with existing notice approaches in several domains (Sections 2 to 4). It is not an empirical study.

## Findings
- Current notice and choice mechanisms such as privacy policies are "often ineffective because they are neither usable nor useful, and are therefore ignored by users" (Abstract).
- A notice should cover how data is collected, used, retained and shared, and what controls users have; the design space gives consistent terminology for the options.
- Constrained devices (mobile, wearable, smart home) make notice and control harder.

## Limits the authors state
It is a conceptual taxonomy based on the literature available up to 2015; effectiveness of any design must be tested separately (Section 5).

## How it differs from ours
- It describes how to tell users about data practices and offer controls. It does not examine whether the stated retention or deletion practices happen. It is background for what "documentation" of a deletion method is (the notices and policies we read in our study).

## Use in our paper
- Cite for the claim that notices are often ignored and that notice and choice is the dominant model, and for terminology on retention and control in notices. Light citation, best in one sentence with Habib and Cranor (2022). Can be dropped if the list needs trimming.
