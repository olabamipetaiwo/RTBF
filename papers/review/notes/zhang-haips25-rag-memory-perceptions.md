## Question
How do users understand LLM memory based on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), how do they use it, and what do they expect of it (Abstract)?

## Method
- Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with 18 users (interviews in Chinese, recruited through an online questionnaire; academic and professional backgrounds; mostly young students), mainly about ChatGPT's memory.

## Findings
- Mental models of how memory works were diverse and often incomplete.
- Users valued personalization but worried about privacy, control and accuracy of what is remembered.
- A concern the authors call lack of epistemological certainty: skepticism that user actions such as deletion have any real effect. P18: "Although I can delete this memory on the client or web end ... I remain skeptical whether it will be deleted from their database." The authors say this undermines the perceived effectiveness of user-facing privacy controls.
- Users wanted granular control over memory (review, edit, delete, categorize; timed deletion) and visibility into what was inferred.

## Limits the authors state
Chinese users under Chinese regulations; participants biased towards young, educated students; mainly ChatGPT, whose memory differs from other products.

## How it differs from ours
- Qualitative study of perceptions. Participants doubt that deletion reaches the provider's database; nobody tested it. It is the same user-side evidence as Chen CHI 2026 from a different population.

## Use in our paper
- Idea only (arXiv copy read; no figures). One clause in the paragraph on users of LLM agents: interviews with users in China also found doubt that deleting a memory deletes it from the provider's database.
