# Habib, Pearman, Wang, Zou, Acquisti, Cranor, Sadeh, Schaub. "It's a scavenger hunt": Usability of Websites' Opt-Out and Data Deletion Choices

ACM CHI 2020 (Honolulu, April 25-30, 2020; the permissions block in the PDF). Pages and DOI as in the manifest from Crossref. File: `4_users/habib-chi20-scavenger-hunt.pdf`. Read the abstract, introduction, related work on deletion, the study design in outline, the limitations, and the parts of the results on expectations and on deletion. I skimmed the email and advertising opt-out results and the design recommendations.

## Question
How usable are the privacy choices that websites offer (email opt-out, advertising opt-out, data deletion) when real users try to find and use them (Abstract)?

## Method
- In-lab usability study with 24 participants, each asked first about expectations of websites' data practices and controls, then to complete two tasks on live websites chosen from nine sites that differ in where the choice sits (account settings or privacy policy) and in mechanism (policy text, or a link in the policy) (Section 3, Table 1).
- Task types: opt out of email, opt out of targeted advertising, or request data deletion; the nine sites were chosen from the authors' earlier 150-website content analysis (Habib et al., SOUPS 2019, in group 2).

## Findings
- Participants "struggled with several aspects", mainly finding and recognising the right page from a navigation menu and knowing what to put in a written request; controls in account settings were easier to use than those in privacy policies, yet many still read help pages or emailed for help (Abstract).
- Participants were skeptical of the effectiveness of website controls even though they knew deletion and opt-outs exist (Introduction).
- Expectations about deletion: some were unaware of deletion controls; about half expected to delete data from their profile and some the whole account; nearly all who mentioned a deletion mechanism said they had used one. When asked what controls they wished websites offered, most named controls over data sharing and deletion.
- A concrete failure: on one site the "remove your personal data" item looked clickable but was not, and a subject-access-request form appeared behind a different button, so most participants were confused about why nothing happened.

## Limits the authors state
Exploratory; 24 participants likely over-represent technically savvy users; the nine websites are not representative of all site types; frequencies of problems cannot be generalised (Limitations).

## How it differs from ours
- A lab study of finding and using the control. It does not check whether the deletion worked. It supports the finding that "available" and "usable" differ, and that users doubt effectiveness.

## Use in our paper
- Cite for the claim that users struggle to find and complete deletion on websites and doubt that deletion works, as user-side background next to the audit evidence (Habib et al. 2019, Rupp et al.). Always give the sample (24).
